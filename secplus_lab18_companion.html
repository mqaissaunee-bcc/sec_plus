📋 Overview

A progression from no protection to broken protection to real protection.

Wireless sends everything over the air, so security is entirely about encryption. This lab walks three tiers using capture files: open/plaintext (you pull an image and a zip straight out of the capture — nothing protects it), WEP (you crack the key with aircrack-ng in seconds, then decrypt and read the traffic), and WPA (encrypted, and not trivially cracked). The arc makes the exam's ranking concrete: never WEP, always WPA2/WPA3.

[THE ONE IDEA] On wireless, the encryption is the security — there's no cable to physically protect. WEP is cryptographically broken and crackable in seconds; treating it as 'some protection' is worse than knowing it's none.

⚠️ Where students get stuck

The analysis snags.

[EXPORT OBJECTS] To pull files from a capture, use File → Export Objects (HTTP/FTP) or follow the right stream. Hunting packet-by-packet wastes time.
[LOADING THE WEP KEY] After aircrack recovers the key, you must enter it in Wireshark's IEEE 802.11 decryption settings to actually read the traffic — cracking and decrypting are two steps.
[KEY FORMAT] WEP keys go in as hex (without colons/spaces depending on the field). A format slip means Wireshark won't decrypt.
[EXPECTING TO CRACK WPA LIKE WEP] You won't — that's the point. WPA resists the instant recovery WEP falls to; a strong passphrase makes offline brute force impractical.
[RIGHT CAPTURE FILE] Three captures, three phases. Open the one the step calls for (httpcapture / WEP / WPA) or the tools behave unexpectedly.

🛡️ The defender's view

Wireless defense is almost entirely 'encrypt it properly, and authenticate.'

WPA2 / WPA3, never WEP/WPA

Breaks: trivial decryption

WEP is broken and WPA is weak. WPA2 (AES-CCMP) is the minimum; WPA3 is better. This is the single most important wireless control.

Strong passphrase (or 802.1X)

Breaks: offline brute force

WPA2-Personal's security rests on the passphrase — long and random defeats offline cracking. Enterprise (WPA2/3-Enterprise, 802.1X/RADIUS) gives per-user credentials instead of one shared key.

Don't rely on hiding

Breaks: SSID/MAC obscurity

Hidden SSIDs and MAC filtering are trivially bypassed and are not encryption. They're obscurity, not security — never a substitute for WPA2/3.

Encrypt the application layer too

Breaks: on-path reading

Even on WPA2, use HTTPS/SFTP so a compromised or open network can't expose data — defense in depth, echoing Lab 22.

Disable WPS

Breaks: PIN brute force

Wi-Fi Protected Setup's PIN is brute-forceable and undermines a strong passphrase. Turn it off.

Segment guest/wireless

Breaks: flat access from the air

Put wireless (especially guest) on its own segment so a compromised Wi-Fi client can't reach internal systems.

✅ Check yourself

1. Why is encryption the whole ballgame for wireless security, more than for wired?

There's no physical cable to protect — Wi-Fi broadcasts over the air, so anyone in range can capture it. The only thing standing between an eavesdropper and your traffic is the encryption, which is why the choice of WEP/WPA/WPA2/WPA3 matters so much.

2. aircrack-ng recovers the WEP key almost instantly. What does that tell you about using WEP today?

WEP is cryptographically broken — its flawed use of initialization vectors leaks the key as traffic accumulates, so it offers effectively no protection. It must never be used; treating it as 'some security' is more dangerous than knowing there's none.

3. WPA2-Personal uses a shared passphrase. What's the attack, and what defeats it?

An attacker captures the WPA handshake and brute-forces the passphrase offline. A long, random passphrase makes that computationally impractical. For stronger protection, WPA2/3-Enterprise (802.1X) replaces the shared key with per-user credentials.

4. A network uses a hidden SSID and MAC filtering 'for security.' Evaluate that.

Both are obscurity, not security — hidden SSIDs are revealed by normal client traffic and MAC addresses are easily spoofed, so both are trivially bypassed. They're no substitute for strong WPA2/WPA3 encryption, which is what actually protects the traffic.
